# Reminiscence Therapy and Occupational Performance in Dementia

## Academic dissertation summary

**Author:** G. Lynn Gable  
**Degree:** Master of Science in Occupational Therapy  
**Institution:** Brunel University, Department of Clinical Sciences, College of Health and Life Sciences  
**Year:** 2020

## Research question

This qualitative dissertation explored how reminiscence therapy may relate to affect, self-concept, and occupational performance among people living with dementia.

## Study design

- Face-to-face, semi-structured interviews
- Nine participants living with dementia in a residential-care setting
- Interviews conducted before and after a four-week intervention period
- Usual-activities and intervention groups
- Hand transcription and thematic analysis

## Summary of reported findings

The dissertation reported that reminiscence sessions appeared to support some areas of function, including insight, perception, mood, and self-concept. The intervention group appeared to describe mood and self-concept in greater detail than the comparison group and to identify occupational barriers more readily. Differences in occupational performance were limited, and the dissertation identified the duration and progression of dementia and participants' life-course factors as possible influences.

These observations are reported as findings from a small qualitative academic study. They do not establish clinical effectiveness or causal impact.

## Limitations and interpretation

The dissertation identifies important limitations, including:
- Small sample size
- Qualitative and subjective measures
- Potential response bias
- Environmental factors between study components
- Limited ability to quantify change
- Need for further research using more specific aims and quantitative approaches

This summary is for academic and portfolio context. It is not clinical advice, a treatment recommendation, or evidence that reminiscence therapy will produce the same outcomes in another population or setting.

## Public-release boundary

The full dissertation is intentionally not included in this public repository. Its appendices include ethics documentation, recruitment and participation materials, consent materials, risk assessment, interview questions, transcripts, approval correspondence, and reflective notes. Those materials require a separate privacy, participant-consent, university/copyright, and publication-rights review.

This repository includes only a high-level summary. No participant records, raw transcripts, consent forms, ethics documents, or controlled research appendices are published.
